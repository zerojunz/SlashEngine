Before: Forward+ scenes finished their colors the way Godot's Compatibility renderer does. Colors were tonemapped as meshes drew, or went through an 8-bit texture for glow, so nothing could be brighter than white. Glow needed a threshold below 1, blended only one way, and ignored Godot's `glow_levels`, `glow_strength`, `glow_mix` and blend modes. Transparent meshes blended in sRGB.

After: Forward+ scenes keep their colors high dynamic range, as Godot 4.6's Forward+ renderer does. A neon sign six times white glows past its edges while a white wall beside it stays as it is. Glow uses Godot's seven levels with their weights, `glow_normalized`, `glow_strength` and all five blend modes (additive, screen, softlight, replace, mix). The tonemappers use Godot 4.6's whites, including AgX's own `tonemap_agx_white` and `tonemap_agx_contrast`. Transparent meshes blend in linear color. Compatibility scenes, and the OpenGL devices, which always draw as Compatibility, draw as before, except that `glow_intensity` defaults to Godot 4.6's 0.3.

How:
- **Shaders.** `mesh.hlsl` and `sky.hlsl` leave Forward+ colors linear (`color_stage` in `src/post.cpp` says which stage a scene is at). The new `shaders/forward_post.hlsl` ports Godot 4.6's glow passes (its Gaussian kernel and Karis-weighted first level) and `tonemap.glsl`: bicubic glow gathering, the blend modes, the tonemappers with allenwp's AgX curve, the exact sRGB curve, adjustments and 1D/3D color correction.
- **Devices.** Vulkan, Metal and Direct3D 12 (`gpu_device.cpp`), WebGPU (`webgpu_device.cpp`) and the software device draw the sky and meshes into an RGBA16F world cleared to the linear clear color. They draw glow into up to seven half-float levels sized as Godot's mipmaps, as many as the levels' weights reach (`forward_glow_plan`), then tonemap over the target before the 2D draws. The software device keeps its world in half floats rounded as a GPU stores them (`round_half`), and `run_forward_glow_pass` and `run_forward_tonemap_pass` are the CPU twins `frame.capture` draws with.
- **Components.** `WorldEnvironment` gains `glow_levels`, `glow_normalized`, `glow_strength`, `glow_mix`, `glow_blend_mode`, `tonemap_agx_white` and `tonemap_agx_contrast`, with Godot 4.6's defaults. Devices refuse non-finite values, negative levels and unknown blend modes.
- **Import.** Godot's `glow_levels/1` to `/7`, `glow_normalized`, `glow_strength`, `glow_mix`, `glow_blend_mode` and AgX settings import onto `WorldEnvironment`.
- **Tests.** The new `tests/test_forward_post.cpp` covers half-float rounding, glow plans, whites, clear colors coming back exactly, linear blending, glow from colors above white (none in Compatibility), strength, levels, all five blend modes, and GPUs matching the software device in three setups. The web tests hold WebGPU to the same pixels. Tests written for Compatibility now say so (`test_post.cpp`, `test_sky.cpp`), and the mesh comparison checks the OpenGL devices against Compatibility's picture.
- **Examples.** The new `examples/glow-3d` is two neon signs on a street at night: their glow, the softlight blend mode, the softest level alone and a threshold above both signs. `post-processing-3d` now sets `gl_compatibility`, which its README describes. Nine 3D examples' outputs move by one step of 255 at a few points.
