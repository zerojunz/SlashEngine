Before: shiny floors, metal and water in 3D scenes reflected only the sky, never the objects around them, and Godot's `Environment` SSR fields and screen-space reflection project setting were reported and dropped on import.

After: a `WorldEnvironment`'s `ssr_enabled` reflects what is on screen in glossy surfaces, as Godot 4.6's Forward+ renderer does, with Godot's max steps, fade in, fade out and depth tolerance fields. project.json's `rendering.environment.screen_space_reflection.half_size` traces at half or full resolution, as Godot's project setting. Vulkan, Metal, Direct3D 12, WebGPU and the software device (so `frame.capture` too) draw it, and Godot projects bring the fields and setting over.

How:
- **Passes.** `ssr.hlsl` holds Godot's SSR compute shaders as fragment passes: at half size the depth is halved keeping each 2x2 pixels' nearest, with the normal and roughness of that pixel (`screen_space_reflection_downsample.glsl`); a pyramid of the nearest depths is built from it (`screen_space_reflection_hiz.glsl`, reading a third column and row from a level of odd size); each pixel's reflected ray steps across the pyramid's cells, down a level where it might hit and up one where it can't, and reads the frame before where it lands, moved to where it is now (`screen_space_reflection.glsl`, its own pass in `ssr_trace.hlsl` since it draws two targets); rougher pixels pick a blurrier level of the reflections' Gaussian pyramid (`screen_space_reflection_filter.glsl`); and at half size the result is resolved to the frame's size along depths and normals (`screen_space_reflection_resolve.glsl`). `ssr_plan` lists the passes, `ssr_uniforms` fills Godot's push constants and scene data (its corrected projection and the reprojection to the last frame's camera), and `run_ssr_pass` is the CPU twin. The passes load texels and filter in code, so every device draws the same values.
- **Applying it.** `mesh.hlsl` and its CPU twin put the reflection over the sky's, faded out from roughness 0.6 to 0.7, as Godot's scene shader does with its SSR texture. Transparent meshes get none, and `gl_compatibility` has no SSR, as in Godot.
- **The frame before.** After the meshes' color pass each device keeps the frame's color at SSR's size (averaged in 2x2 pixels at half size) with the camera it was seen from, per target, for the next frame's trace, as Godot's `copy_internal_texture_to_last_frame`. The first frame on a target draws its color once without SSR to have one, so one-off captures reflect too. The history is dropped when the target is destroyed or a frame draws without SSR.
- **Devices.** The SDL GPU and WebGPU devices draw the prepass, the passes and the last frame into targets kept between frames and sized by the plan, and bind the result to the mesh pass. The software device runs the CPU twins on the same prepass. The GPU and software devices match within a few levels on every setting.
- **Fields.** `WorldEnvironment` gains Godot's five `ssr_*` fields and defaults; `scene3d::environment` copies them into `render::Ssr3D`. `Renderer::prepare` takes `render::SsrSettings`, which games, sessions and `frame.capture` read from project.json.
- **Import.** An `Environment`'s SSR fields now convert with the rest, and `rendering/environment/screen_space_reflection/half_size` becomes `rendering.environment.screen_space_reflection.half_size`.
- **Tests.** `test_ssr.cpp`: plans at half and full size, uniforms as Godot's push constants and scene data (projection, inverse and reprojection), the nearest-depth pyramid on a frame of odd size, the settings, a floor reflecting a wall and a square on every Forward+ device at half and full size, in perspective and orthogonal projections and with the camera moving between frames, rough floors, Compatibility unchanged, and the GPU matching the software device. `test_scene3d.cpp`: fields and settings reaching the frame, a captured frame reflecting a glowing cube in a polished floor, and project.json's setting checked and read. `test_import_godot.cpp`: the fields and project setting.
- **Example.** The new `examples/ssr-3d` reflects a crate, a pillar and a wall in a polished hall floor, roughens the floor with `component.set` until the reflections fade, then turns SSR off.
