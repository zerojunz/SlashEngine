Before: a 3D scene was exposed the same way whatever it showed. A camera walking from a dark tunnel into daylight saw the daylight burn white, and nothing like Godot's `CameraAttributesPractical` existed, so Godot projects lost their exposure settings on import. Emission energy was applied before the color was made linear, so a material's `emission_energy_multiplier` of 6 shone about 66 times white rather than 6.

After: a camera's `CameraAttributesPractical` (or the `WorldEnvironment`'s, for cameras without their own) exposes the 3D world as Godot 4.6's does. `exposure_multiplier` brightens lights, emission, the background and the sky. With `auto_exposure_enabled`, Forward+ measures each frame and adapts to it over game time, so daylight starts white and settles to its blue in a few seconds, and stepping back into the tunnel goes dark before the eyes adjust. Emission is the linear color times its energy, as in Godot, so the glow examples now shine as brightly as their READMEs say.

How:
- **Component.** `CameraAttributesPractical` with Godot's fields and defaults: `exposure_sensitivity`, `exposure_multiplier`, `auto_exposure_enabled`, `auto_exposure_scale`, `auto_exposure_speed` and the min and max sensitivities. `scene3d::camera_attributes` picks the camera's own, else the first `WorldEnvironment`'s. `scene3d::environment` applies the multiplier where Godot's exposure normalization does (lights, sky suns, the background and sky energy, emission), and not to ambient color or fog, and turns the sensitivities into brightnesses (sensitivity × 0.125 / `exposure_sensitivity`). A zero `exposure_sensitivity` is reported and auto exposure is skipped.
- **Measuring.** `forward_post.hlsl` gains Godot's `luminance_reduce.glsl` passes: each pixel's brightest channel averaged in 8×8 blocks, then the blocks, down to one R32F value that the device keeps between frames. The last pass moves `auto_exposure_adjust` of the way from the previous brightness and clamps it to the min and max. The tonemap and glow's first level are exposed by scale over brightness. `luminance_plan` and `run_luminance_pass` are the CPU twins. Vulkan, Metal and Direct3D 12, WebGPU and the software device run the same passes.
- **Game time.** `Renderer::prepare` takes the game time and turns `auto_exposure_speed` into each frame's adjust. A new version starts the measurement over when auto exposure turns on, when the camera follows other attributes, or when time goes back (as after a restore). `frame.capture`'s `time` carries it from one capture to the next.
- **Emission.** `mesh.hlsl` and its CPU twin in `mesh.cpp` make the emission color linear and then multiply by the energy. The glow tests' dim square is now 2.5 times white, which is about what it drew before.
- **Import.** A Godot `WorldEnvironment`'s `camera_attributes` and a `Camera3D`'s `attributes` that are a `CameraAttributesPractical` become the component. `CameraAttributesPhysical` and the depth of field fields are reported.
- **Tests.** `test_forward_post.cpp`: block reduction and the final pass's smoothing and clamping; dark frames brightened and bright ones darkened; smoothing, new versions, the floor and ceiling and exposed glow on every Forward+ device; and the GPUs matching the software device over three frames. `test_scene3d.cpp`: which attributes win, the multiplier, sensitivities, the zero-sensitivity problem and versions over time; a captured frame adapting over game time. `test_import_godot.cpp`: both import paths.
- **Examples.** The new `examples/auto-exposure-3d` walks from a torch-lit tunnel into daylight and back, then changes `auto_exposure_scale`. `glow-3d`, `post-processing-3d` and `fbx-models` outputs change where emission is above 1, and the glow-3d and post-processing-3d READMEs quote the new colors.
- **Docs.** scene-format.md's new `CameraAttributesPractical` section and the emission row, godot-import.md, AGENTS.md and the roadmap.
